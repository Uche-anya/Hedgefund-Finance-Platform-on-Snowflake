# PepsiCo dividend candidate: 5 December 2025

**Status: approved as a simulated dividend entitlement on 6 October 2026;
no cash confirmation or NAV publication.** The
machine-checked files are in
`data/two_year_action_review/pep_2025-12-05_evidence_v1/`.
Run `scripts/prepare_pep_dividend_review.py` to reproduce them. The input
hashes are in `evidence.json`.

PepsiCo's [dividend history](https://www.pepsico.com/investors/dividend-information)
and [19 November 2025 announcement](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/pepsico-declares-quarterly-dividend)
say $1.4225 per common share, record date 5 December 2025, payable 6 January
2026. Those fields match the saved Massive event. The issuer pages do not
state an ex-date.

The provider says the ex-date is 5 December. A
[Frankfurt exchange notice](https://www.cashmarket.deutsche-boerse.com/cash-en/Stay-Informed/newsroom/Xetra-Frankfurt-Newsboard/XFRA-DIVIDEND-INTEREST-INFORMATION-05.12.2025-US7134481081-4838152)
also sets 5 December for its listing of PepsiCo's US ISIN. The
[Nasdaq ordinary-dividend rule](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/rulebook/nasdaq/rules/nasdaq-equity-11)
normally sets a business-day record date as the ex-date, with exceptions.
The $1.4225 dividend is well below 25% of the saved $146.91 prior close,
which is another condition of Nasdaq's normal rule.
Together these support the provider date, but the Frankfurt notice is not a
Nasdaq event notice. A reviewer must accept a documented rule-based policy or
obtain an event-specific US listing source before approval.

Our dated security map calls this `NB_EQ_0019`, Nasdaq ticker `PEP`, common
stock, share-class FIGI `BBG001S695T1`. Its provider identity check was made
on 10 January 2025, before this dividend. The issuer announcement names PEP
common stock in November 2025, but it does not independently state the FIGI.
The review should confirm that the mapped security is still the same share
class on the ex-date.

The saved close has these PepsiCo shares at the **4 December close**. They
equal the opening quantities used for the 5 December candidate:

The negative amount is a possible short-dividend payable; the positive amount
is a possible long-dividend receivable. The calculation is internally
consistent, but the accounts and their positions are simulated. It is not
independent broker or custodian confirmation.

`conditional_nav.csv` shows what approval **would** do: move these amounts
from pending to reviewed NAV on 198 market days, or 396 account-day rows.
It does not change illustrative NAV. The 6 January pay date is only the
issuer's scheduled date. The simulated accounts have no payment confirmation
for this dividend, so approving the entitlement would **not** create a cash
receipt or payment.
The candidate calculation can have half-cent precision; any later simulated
cash payment needs an explicit rounding rule and separate reconciliation.
